What was Life Like Here?


(The picture above is a monument memoriaizing the Third Crossing)

Originally, there was no settlement where Gladstone stands today but it was used as a trade route crossing over the Whitemud River for Indigenous people, Metis and European Settlers. There were three different crossings in the area 1st Crossing (modern day Westbourne), 2nd Crossing (modern day Woodside) and the Third Crossing (Modern Day Gladstone)

*Fun Fact - The Care Home in Gladstone still holds the name Third Crossing Manor and sits only a short distance away from the original crossing location.

Life around Third Crossing at that time was very different than it is now. As it was part of an established trade route, the area was used for hunting (especially Bison), fishing (the whitemud river has always been a good place to fish), and gathering of wild plants used for various things.
